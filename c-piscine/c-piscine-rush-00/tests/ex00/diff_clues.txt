  This layer replays thousands of sizes against the reference: every rectangle
  up to 40x40, long 1-, 2- and 3-wide bands, the widths that sit on a counter or
  buffer ceiling (64, 80, 100, 128, 256, 512, 1024, 4096 and their neighbours),
  and a seeded random tail. The divergences printed above are the first ones
  found, one line each.

  How to read a divergence: the first two columns are x and y, the rest is the
  rectangle with '\n' marking each line ending -- so a trailing space, a missing
  final newline and an extra row are all visible in the text itself.

  Then ask which FAMILY of sizes fails, not which single case:
    * only sizes with a 1 in them          -> the row/column that is first and
                                              last at the same time
    * only sizes with a 2 in them          -> a fill loop that cannot run zero times
    * every size, off by one character     -> a bound on the column loop
    * every size, off by one line          -> a bound on the row loop
    * wide-but-short but not tall-but-thin -> x and y are not doing the job you think
    * fine until 128 or 256 wide           -> the type of the variable you count with
